Rain ahead as the sea cools sharply

This week, the sky deserves as much attention as the sea: AEMET is pointing to several rainy days from Wednesday onwards. Offshore, the water has made a clear move towards autumn, while a newly found deep coral forest offers a reminder of how much remains unseen below.

These are days for choosing the right weather window, checking the forecast before casting off and taking the sea at its own pace. When the breaks arrive, there will still be fine reasons to sail, dive or fish from Puerto Banús.

The water averaged 22.4 °C, still pleasant for swimming and diving in suitable exposure protection, though the seasonal cooling is now noticeable. With a maximum wave of 1.1 m and a top gust of 38 km/h, it has been a fortnight for picking calmer sea windows; first quarter moon falls on 19 October.

What the data say
Water cooled by 1.5 °C, three times the usual seasonal fall

Copernicus Marine water temperature fell from 23.1 °C to 21.6 °C between 29 September and 13 October, a drop of 1.5 °C. Across the same dates in the previous three years, the average change was -0.5 °C, the normal seasonal pattern.

The sharpest two-day fall was -0.6 °C from 5 October to 7 October, with easterly wind at 8 km/h and north-easterly wind at 13 km/h. In the longer record, easterlies tend to warm the water on the following day, while westerlies tend to cool it; this fortnight’s cooling has been quicker than usual.
