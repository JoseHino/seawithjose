Sea temperature held at 23.1 °C against October’s usual fall

The standout this fortnight is that the water finished at 23.1 °C, exactly where it started. That is unusual for October and gives us a fine opportunity to keep enjoying the sea, provided we keep an eye on the wind before making a plan.

I am also struck by how dynamic the Mediterranean is proving to be. There is always something worth noticing on a sea this close to home; if you would like to see it from the water, I will be in Puerto Banús.

With an average water temperature of 22.4 °C and a maximum wave of 1.2 m, there were plenty of pleasant days for swimming and diving in a light wetsuit. Gusts of up to 42 km/h made timing and shelter important, while the first quarter on 19 October leads towards the full moon on 26 October for anglers watching the tides.

What the data say
Water ended unchanged, rather than the usual -1.0 °C October drop

Copernicus Marine water temperature went from 23.1 °C to 23.1 °C between 1 and 15 October, a change of +0.0 °C. Over the same dates in the previous three years, the average change was -1.0 °C, the usual seasonal decline.

The sharpest rise was +1.5 °C from 13 to 15 October, with an easterly of 23 km/h on the final day. In the historical data, strong easterlies are associated with an average next-day change of +0.15 °C; easterlies and westerlies shift surface water, though these Copernicus Marine figures come from satellite and model data, not a measurement in Marbella.
