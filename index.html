        How I work out the score
        • Sailing: the sweet spot is 13 to 28 km/h of wind (force 3 to 4) with a calm sea. Under 5 km/h we motor; from 34 km/h, or with a choppy sea, the score drops fast.
        • Diving: the state of the sea decides: up to small waves, good; choppy, so-so; anything more, no diving. Wind over 20 km/h and swell take points off.
        • Fishing: small waves at most and wind under 22 km/h.
        • All three: thunderstorms, fog and rain take points off. With an AEMET coastal warning or a gale, the score stays at 2 or below.

        It’s a guide based on the forecast, not on the real sea: I make the final call at the pontoon on the day, and if we can’t go out, we find another day.

        Diving in Marbella

        Underwater, Marbella is another city

        Calm, shallow water, seagrass meadows, reefs and wrecks with centuries of history. Whether it’s your first dive or your hundredth, there’s a plan for you.

        Your first dive

        Calm, shallow water and me right beside you the whole time. No experience needed — just curiosity.

        Get certified

        FEDAS · CMAS courses: from One Star Diver (B1E), which lets you dive with a buddy down to 25 metres, to advanced levels and specialties. Recognised worldwide through CMAS.

        Wreck off San Pedro Alcántara

        The Galleon: the warship Fernando, 1760

        For decades everyone called it “the Galleon” and said it was French. In 2026 the Andalusian Institute of Historical Heritage solved the mystery: it is the Fernando, a Spanish ship of the line built in Ferrol in 1750–51 and lost in 1760.

        Its remains lie just six metres down: three centuries of history within reach of any diver, even on a try dive.

        Depth
        about 6 m
        Level
        everyone, from a try dive
        You’ll see
        cannons, frames and the life on the wreck

        The Beans Wreck — Punta Carnero

        The Dina, a 109-metre freighter that ran aground in an easterly storm in 1983 carrying tinned beans. Today it’s a reef full of life between 8 and 12 metres.
